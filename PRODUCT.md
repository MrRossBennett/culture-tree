# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Culture Tree serves casual culture explorers and serious curators or researchers.

Casual explorers arrive with a seed: an album, film, book, artist, era, scene, or feeling. They want a guided way to follow references, influences, adjacent works, and surprising cultural connections without needing to know the canon already.

Serious curators and researchers use Culture Tree to shape, compare, preserve, and share cultural maps. They care about provenance, specificity, and the quality of the connective tissue between branches.

## Product Purpose

Culture Tree helps people grow curated maps of connected cultural references from a single starting idea. The product turns a seed into a navigable Culture Tree across music, films, books, artists, people, places, scenes, and influences.

Success means the user feels they have discovered a meaningful path through culture, not merely received a list of recommendations. The generated tree should feel authored, connected, and worth returning to.

## Brand Personality

Culture Tree is curious, cinematic, and discerning.

The tone should feel like a premium editorial collection: thoughtful, cultured, visually composed, and quietly confident. It should invite exploration without flattening the subject into generic recommendations.

## Anti-references

Culture Tree should not feel like generic AI SaaS, a streaming-service clone, an academic archive, a productivity workspace, a crypto graph explorer, or a moodboard app.

Avoid loud AI novelty, disposable recommendation feeds, sterile database interfaces, gamified growth loops, and anything that makes cultural discovery feel algorithmically cheap.

## Design Principles

Lead with curation, not automation. The AI should feel like a careful guide in service of cultural discovery, not the product's whole personality.

Make connections feel authored. Every branch should suggest why it belongs, what it opens up, and how it changes the shape of the tree.

Balance welcome and depth. Casual explorers should feel invited immediately; serious curators should find enough structure and specificity to trust the product.

Treat cultural material with premium editorial care. Typography, rhythm, imagery, metadata, and labels should feel selected, not dumped.

Keep the tree language intact. Use Seed, Branch, Culture Tree, Grow Branch, Public Tree, Private Tree, and the project vocabulary from CONTEXT.md instead of graph or node terminology.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for core flows.

Support keyboard-friendly generation, navigation, filtering, dialogs, settings, and tree actions.

Respect reduced-motion preferences, especially in tree reveal, hover, loading, and transition states.

Do not rely on hue alone to communicate type, status, selection, ownership, privacy, allowance state, or errors.

Preserve readable contrast and comfortable text sizing across the premium editorial styling, including serif typography, muted palettes, and image-backed branch cards.
